## 03:40 UTC -- the maintainer's questions found a simpler architecture

Working the nine decisions one at a time, the maintainer stopped on "the
stage" and asked how a copy taken at exit could be safe if the emulator
might still be writing -- and caught a sentence of mine that was wrong as
worded. The precise answer (the emulator that exited is gone; the copy
guards against the *next* emulator during a long upload) led to the
better question: why allow a launch during the upload at all? Cancelling
a sync the player can see is counterintuitive. So: the launch waits, the
sync bounds itself to a few seconds and fails with a connectivity outcome
past that, never minutes, time-based bounds (D-CLOUD-109). With nothing
writing during the upload, the frozen copy of the new save has no job;
the stage is the last-agreed cache and nothing more (D-CLOUD-110).
Decisions 1-4 before it: D-CLOUD-105 (fleet setting; OFF keeps a
transaction copy -- the council's reading rejected), 106 (prune order,
the 90-day cap dropped), 107 (copy never move), 108 (the one fallback
fetch).

The two numbers D-CLOUD-098 names: **UI to a game's first frame 0.63 s**,
identical on SFTP, WebDAV and S3 — nothing cloud is on the launch path at
rest. **Game to game 1.01 s**, because ten launches out of ten cancelled the
exit sync (D-CLOUD-076 as built). Under D-CLOUD-109 the same two presses cost
about 2.1 s, from the two measured halves.

What the measurement taught, beyond the numbers:

- **"The screen changed" is not "the game drew".** The interface tears its
  window down at 0.21 s and the screen goes black; RetroArch draws at 0.63 s.
  A cell that watches for the first change reports the handover. The exit
  test's readiness rule (200 CPU ticks) is the other error, three seconds the
  other way. The cell needs all three tests: not the carousel, not blank, not
  before the process exists.
- **A stalled endpoint hangs the exit sync for 320.9 s**, identical on SFTP
  and WebDAV — `--timeout 30s` × `--low-level-retries 10`. The card reads
  STARTING... unchanged, the screensaver blanks the screen at 300.6 s, and the
  outcome lands at 320.9 s behind a blank screen. Nobody ever sees how it
  ended. `--contimeout` never fired in any cell, on any cloud: the bound that
  exists is not the one doing the work, and the retry count is the lever.
- **`has_default_route` is "any default route, either family".** Deleting the
  IPv4 default leaves the IPv6 one and the exit sync completed normally while
  the cell believed it was testing an offline device. Also: `ip route del
  default` deletes ONE match. Both cost a whole conditions run.
- **A diagnostic must not be able to end a measurement.** `cut -c` on the
  guest counts bytes, so a trimmed UTF-8 log line ended mid-character and
  `subprocess(text=True)` raised out of a three-cell run, one cell of which
  was five minutes long.
