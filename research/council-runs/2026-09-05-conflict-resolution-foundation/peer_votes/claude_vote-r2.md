**Winner: `gpt-revised_plan-r2.md`.**

## Why, in the brief's order

**1. Progress protection and reversibility.** All four plans converge on the same safety core — sha256 identity (D-CLOUD-030), a manifest three-way test, `--backup-dir` in both directions, receipts/tombstones only for recorded intent, fail-closed on unexplained absence, a checked ES merge adapter, retention on by default. The differences that matter are at the edges of that core, and they cut decisively for `gpt-revised_plan-r2.md`:

- **It has no stated silent-overwrite path.** Its game-exit path (§5.2) reads cloud evidence for the *changed units only* before uploading, and leaves anything it cannot classify pending. `kimi-revised_plan-r2.md` §4.3 instead uploads changed saves with `--backup-dir` after consulting a *cached* foreign-manifest claim, and states the residual honestly: "a fork created since the last full pass can be overwritten on the exit path." Trace that residual one step further with kimi's own classifier: the other device's next full pass sees L=H_b, C=H_a, A=H_b → "cloud changed → download". The fork is never surfaced as a conflict to either player; both heads survive only in dated siblings and audit lines. Nothing is *lost*, but it is last-uploader-wins with no prompt — a recency resolution in the sense `00-problem-statement.md` and #11 forbid, recoverable only if someone thinks to look. The brief says a plan with one silent-overwrite path loses to a plainer plan with none; gpt pays one listing on the changed-save exit (the nothing-changed fast path is untouched) to remove it, and refines D-CLOUD-028 openly to do so. `gemini-revised_plan-r2.md` also lists the remote first, but its known-unknown 2 proposes falling back to "fast-path upload only" if the budget is exceeded — which would reintroduce exactly this path on hashless backends.
- **It treats undo as a console-first V1 requirement.** §8: "A directory of bytes recoverable only through SSH is not a complete console-first escape hatch," and it requires "a minimal native recovery route for retained decisions." No other plan names a player-facing undo surface. `kimi-revised_plan-r2.md` builds the retention store (§4.6) but never says how a player gets a save back out of `/storage/.cache/cloud_sync/discarded/`; `gemini-revised_plan-r2.md` and `mistral-revised_plan-r2.md` likewise stop at the store.
- **Its agreement rules are the tightest.** Agreement is written only after verified upload, verified download, verified equality, or a verified recorded operation, and is scoped to remote identity, cloud prefix, local root and storage identity (§4.1). Kimi matches the write rules (§5.9) and scopes to (remote, sync root); gemini and mistral do not specify.

**2. Grounding.** gpt labels every claim source-visible / decided / proposed / unmeasured, and its corrections check out against the embedded ES sources: `onGameEnded()` renumbers only when `saveStateInfo` exists and, on the `racommands` branch, only for slot ≥ 0 (`es/SaveState.cpp`, `es/FileData.cpp.launchGame-excerpt-l740-850.cpp`); `isEnabled()` requires `retroarch` (`es/SaveStateRepository.cpp`); rclone's own exit 3/4 pass through `clean_exit` into the UI's SKIPPED meanings and both scripts return only the saves-phase status (`cloud_backup`, `cloud_restore`, `es/ThreadedCloudSync.cpp`); the boot pair runs backup even when restore fails (`autostart/102-cloud-saves`). Critically, it refuses to inherit the council's unmeasured claims — `--backup-dir` is "not proven atomic-equivalent or zero-round-trip," and it declines to promise a "two-spawn maximum." That refusal is uncomfortable but correct: the corpus only shows `--backup-dir` used in mirror mode (`cloud_backup`, D-CLOUD-014), never under a two-writer race. Kimi is equally well grounded and its §6 "corpus-settled vs council-agreed" table is the best single artefact in any plan, but kimi does describe `--backup-dir` as "zero extra spawns" without the cost caveat and assigns itself register IDs (D-CLOUD-032…036) that are the maintainer's to assign.

**3. Implementability.** This is where gpt is weakest and kimi strongest. gpt's V1 must-ship list (§13) is long, and several records ("operation record," "generation / parent observation," "possession / placement") are described semantically without concrete file formats or paths. Kimi gives an implementer `ops.log` at a named path with two named ES hook points (`copyToSlot(move=true)`, `SaveState::remove()`), the temp-name-in-the-real-save-directory staging rule that follows from `makeStateFilename` deriving from the source's parent, and batch-reserved slots. gpt says "recognize ES's recorded moves" without saying how ES records them. Both are heavy V1s; kimi is lighter because it defers #10 and resolution receipts. On this criterion alone kimi would win — but the brief orders the criteria, and gpt's extra weight buys the closure of the exit-path residual and the undo route, which is exactly what criterion 1 and the amendment demand.

**4. Sequencing.** Both gpt (P0–P5, then behaviour gates) and kimi (gates 0–9) put the cheap invalidating experiments first: repair `tools/cloud-round-trip` before anything destructive (both note it overwrites `rclone.conf` and never restores it, and that its dated-archive assertions cannot pass against the embedded uploader), the bisync matrix with "question zero" being what `--conflict-resolve none` does to names and bytes, the boot-race reproducer, the #19 same-chipset control before cross-chipset. Kimi's ordering is slightly crisper (a no-hardware gate 0, a VM counterexample gate 1). gemini's five-item proof order is sound but skeletal; mistral's gate table claims "≤ 2 spawns, ≤ 5 s on H700" as a pass condition for a budget nobody has measured.

**5. Honesty about unknowns and reopened rows.** gpt is the only plan that names D-CLOUD-014 as needing a refinement row, which is right: any propagation of a recorded deletion — even copy-verify-delete into a sibling — touches "a backup never deletes by default," and kimi's receipts do it without citing the row. gpt closes all eleven known unknowns (§11), states the mixed-version-writer limitation plainly (§9.4: a new device cannot stop an old binary elsewhere from running its old overwrite), and lists its corpus gaps in `corpus.provenance.json`. Kimi is equally honest and closes all eleven as well. gemini closes four. mistral states "no unmeasured claims" in a document that contains several.

## Why not the others, specifically

- **`kimi-revised_plan-r2.md`** is the runner-up by a narrow margin and the plan most of the synthesis should be drawn from for *mechanism*. It loses on the exit-path residual described above, on the missing player-facing undo surface, and on not flagging D-CLOUD-014. Its deferral of #10 to its own futro is defensible but moves a milestone child (`00-problem-statement.md` lists #10 among the children) out of the drop without the maintainer's word.
- **`gemini-revised_plan-r2.md`** is correct in nearly everything it says and is the easiest to read, but it is a summary, not a foundation: no classifier table, no agreement-write rules, no lock order, no capture-is-unconditional rule, no `RCLONEOPTS`-without-`--filter-from` hazard, four of eleven known unknowns. An implementer adopting it as written would have to design most of the engine. Its one concrete harness fix — "remove the `rclone.conf` overwrite" — is mis-aimed: the harness overwrites `rclone.conf` *deliberately* to point the device at the QA backend; the defect is that it does not preserve and restore the original.
- **`mistral-revised_plan-r2.md`** is not standalone. It builds "from `claude-revised_plan.md`" with section citations ("claude §3.4," "§3.6") to a document that is not injected, says its conflict table is "lifted verbatim" without including it, cites the retention default against D-CLOUD-027 (the audit-log row, not the IA/D-CLOUD-024 row it actually refines), declares D-CLOUD-029 "withdrawn" — the register is append-only and `gemini-revised_plan-r2.md`, which it credits for the "procedural closure," in fact keeps D-CLOUD-029 standing — and keeps "conservative resurrection" as V1 deletion policy, the posture that cannot terminate against D-CLOUD-030's compaction (kimi's C1 trace, verified against `es/SaveStateRepository.cpp` and `es/SaveState.cpp`). It would not be safe to hand to a builder.

## What the winner needs to satisfy the amendment

gpt is already the closest plan to the maintainer's restatement — retention on by default (§8), a native recovery route in V1 (§8), fail-closed on unexplained absence without abandoning recorded-deletion propagation (§4.2–4.3), which is precisely the balance the amendment asks for. Three amendments, each cheap:

1. **Design the undo surface, don't just require it.** The primary use case is "I pressed the wrong side and want it back." The recovery route should be a single row (`GAME SETTINGS > CLOUD SETTINGS > UNDO LAST CONFLICT DECISION`, or an UNDO on the wizard's done page) that swaps the applied version with its retained loser for that unit and re-runs the same verified apply machinery. One step, per unit, most-recent decision first. Anything deeper is #25.
2. **Trim depth to one step back.** The narrow resolution receipt (§4.4) and the manifest "generation / parent observation" (§3.3) are lineage machinery beyond the one step the maintainer said matters. Keep the frozen apply plan as the journal (that *is* the undo record); defer receipts and generation tracking to V2 unless the cross-device convergence test (§10.3 item 4) shows they are needed. This is `kimi-revised_plan-r2.md` §7's position and it is the right one under the amendment.
3. **Keep edge-case experiments as tests, not gates on the primary path.** §12's cloned-card, ES-crash-with-live-emulator, case-folding and same-second-collision cases should each produce a fail-closed response and an audit line, but none should block activation of the ordinary single-player resolve-and-undo flow. §3.4's "cloned-card experiment is mandatory" should be reworded to "mandatory before multi-device is declared supported."

## Remaining defects in the winner that must be fixed before it is built

1. **Unnamed formats and paths.** The operation record, retirement record, apply plan, and agreement file need concrete JSON shapes and `/storage/.cache/cloud_sync/` paths before #22 starts; without them a builder invents them. Take `agreed.json` from `docs/save-manifest-schema.md` §7 and kimi's `ops.log` and apply-record sketch as the starting points.
2. **The changed-save exit budget is unpriced.** §5.2 adds an evidence read to every exit that changed a save. gpt is right not to invent a number, but the D-CLOUD-028 refinement cannot go to the maintainer until P5 measures one changed SRAM and one state+PNG on the H700 against Dropbox and the QA WebDAV. If the read pushes a one-save exit well past the ~7 s the corpus already tolerates, the fallback must be "queue as pending and say so," never the unguarded upload.
3. **`--backup-dir` under a two-writer race is a hypothesis the whole safety story rests on.** §6.2 says so; §6.3 gives the outcome tree. The two-H700 race fixture (kill after each destructive stage; both heads present, one canonical, one siblinged; same-second name collision) must run before multi-device is claimed, and the result written into the register row that refines D-CLOUD-014.
4. **The undo surface is a requirement without a design** (see above). It is the primary use case and cannot be left to #23's discretion.
5. **Scope trim.** Defer resolution receipts and manifest generation tracking to V2 per the amendment, and reclassify §12's identity/cloned-card handling from "mandatory" to "before multi-device support is declared." This is the least damaging way to bring the plan's weight down to what a small team can build on a busybox handheld without weakening anything on the single-player resolve-and-undo path.
6. **#10 in the drop needs the maintainer's explicit word** given kimi's deferral argument: gpt's reasoning (a flat namespace collides locally before any reconciler sees it) is correct, and D-CLOUD-017 stands, but the six proof obligations in §9.1 are real work; the maintainer should choose between "in this drop, last" and "own futro" with the cost stated.
