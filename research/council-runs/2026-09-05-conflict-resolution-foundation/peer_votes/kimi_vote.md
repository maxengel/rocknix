**Winner: `claude-revised_plan.md` (member: claude).**

## Reasoning

All four plans converge on the same skeleton — keep D-CLOUD-030/031, demote bisync to a contract-tested spike, wrap ES's merge primitives in a checked adapter, add tombstones and save units, fix the boot race, never inherit `RCLONEOPTS`. The vote turns on which plan is safest *and* most buildable as written, in that order.

**Against `mistral-revised_plan.md`:** mistral's is the weakest grounded. It states as settled fact two things the corpus does not establish — that bisync "demands `--resync` on first runs and filter changes" and that it "is blind to SRAM changes on hashless backends." Both are hypotheses the futro and claude's plan correctly label unverified pending the spike. Its D-CLOUD-029 "replacement" stopgap (disable boot restore, keep the exit upload) overclaims: the exit upload is `copy` with no `--update`, so the later of two offline devices still overwrites the other's cloud copy — both copies survive only incidentally, one per device, and cross-device pickup is wholly broken meanwhile. It also says "Replaced" for a register row, where the register is append-only. It is a direction statement, not a buildable foundation: no conflict table, no deletion semantics, no trigger channel, no capture lifecycle, no migration plan.

**Against `gemini-revised_plan.md`:** gemini's is honest and its concession ledger is exemplary, but it is a review summary, not a foundation. Its load-bearing section names the right four things (manifest detector, merge contract, lifecycle isolation, grouped resolution) at a level of abstraction that would need another full design pass before anyone could build it. Its one distinctive contribution — the one-way stopgap — has the same gloss as mistral's (the exit upload still clobbers the cloud copy; "no data is lost" holds only because each device keeps its own copy locally) and it regresses the maintainer's only workflow for the entire build window. It surfaces no unknown-unknowns of its own and its hardware list is four items with no ordering logic.

**Against `gpt-revised_plan.md`:** this was the hardest call. gpt's plan is the most rigorous document in the set — its defense of the `getNextFreeSlot()` −99 finding walks the embedded code line by line and is correct; its §4.7 protected-publication and resolution-receipt protocol is the strongest answer any plan gives to two-devices-online; its capture lifecycle (pre-session hashes, ES move/delete receipts, "equal hashes ≠ move") is the most careful; and its gate structure's distinction between substrate experiments and adversarial tests of the built artifact is a genuine insight claude's plan lacks. I do not pick it, for three reasons. First, buildability: §4.7 is a small distributed-systems protocol — protected remote areas, publication receipts, competing-head reader logic, idempotence identifiers — to be built in bash against 69 backends inside a 5-second watched exit budget; its own degradation path ("upload protected candidates, leave reconciliation pending") changes the exit sync's product semantics in ways the plan leaves unresolved. Second, proportionality: the corpus records the maintainer *explicitly accepting* the never-concurrent risk for this drop with the audit log as tripwire; claude's contract (pre-upload check + `--backup-dir` retention + anomaly report) is already strictly stronger than that accepted posture, while gpt's builds well beyond it. Third, the brief's own weighting: "safest and most buildable… not the most impressive or most thorough." On criterion 1 both plans have no silent-overwrite path under the stated model; gpt is better only in the model-violation case, where claude is still non-destructive (bytes retained, anomaly reported, manual recovery). On criterion 3 claude is clearly ahead.

**Why claude wins:** it absorbs essentially every validated finding from all four Step-1 analyses with an explicit concession/defense/adoption ledger; it corrects peers' misreadings against the embedded source (gemini's −99 rebuttal, kimi's ping-pong precondition) rather than passing them along; every unverifiable claim is marked [K] with the experiment named; the migration is additive at every step with a shadow mode that measures detector correctness against the legacy paths before anything is replaced; the D-CLOUD-028 refinement keeps the zero-spawn no-change path and budgets the changed path; the register mechanics are precise (refine/endorse/propose, new rows citing old, #10's re-sequencing flagged as the maintainer's call); and the hardware ordering is operational, time-boxed, and starts by repairing the broken harness. It is the plan most likely to actually ship without a silent-overwrite path.

## Dissent — what the losing plans have that the winner does not fully absorb

**From `gpt-revised_plan.md`:**

1. **The protected-publication protocol (§4.7).** The biggest non-absorbed safeguard. Claude's concurrency contract retains overwritten bytes via `--backup-dir` and reports the anomaly, but the racing loser's next pass still silently downloads the winner over its own newer local progress (row 4), with manual recovery. Gpt's device-attributable, non-overwriting publications plus reader consideration of competing heads turn that race into a surfaced conflict. At minimum its guarantee — "overlapping writers must not erase the only copy of a competing head" — should be adopted as a stated requirement, and the resolution-receipt publication (which claude has only as a recorded-loser list) should carry gpt's exact-operand-hash binding.
2. **Two kinds of proof.** Substrate/protocol experiments before implementation, then *the same adversarial cases replayed against the built artifacts* (Gate 6, cutover step 7). Claude's §7 is substrate-only; shadow mode partially covers this but is not the same as re-running the interruption and race cases against the shipped code.
3. **Lock discipline during the walkthrough.** Do not hold the cloud lock while the player thinks; freeze a hash-bound plan, release, reacquire and revalidate at COMPLETE. Claude's "one lock held across plan and apply" contradicts its own recheck and should be replaced with gpt's explicit semantics.
4. **Capture depth.** Pre-session hashes plus ES move/delete receipts, and the honesty rule that equal hashes establish equal bytes, not move-versus-copy — leave lineage unknown rather than manufacture a predecessor. Stronger than claude's R5 possession-record rule alone.
5. **Lifecycle-gate nuance.** Background operations may inspect and upload *sealed staging* while a game runs; only mutation of live data is blocked. Claude's blanket "no reconcile touches the save tree while a game runs" forgoes safe idle-time uploads.
6. **Typed outcomes and the journal.** "Incomplete apply/recovery pending" and "verification failure" as first-class outcomes; the transaction journal is not rotated with `cloud_audit.log`; an "intent" audit line is never written as "resolved." Claude's exit-code set (0/3/4/5/6/1) has no recovery-pending state.
7. **`remote_hash` timing precision.** A manifest uploaded in a pass cannot contain evidence learned after that pass; store post-transfer observations locally and publish later; never require an extra upload just to make the field non-null.
8. **Unique unknown-unknowns:** "no default route" falsely suppressing a LAN-reachable remote (a direct hit on the shipped exit-4 heuristic); a successfully loaded savestate later corrupting the battery save (cross-unit dependency); a reused basename associating a state with the wrong ROM; an absent core producing a downloaded-but-invisible state; and the mixed-version fleet boundary — new code cannot stop an old binary on another device from overwriting, which claude's cutover never addresses.

**From `gemini-revised_plan.md`:**

9. **The one-way stopgap as an interim operating precaution.** Whatever its weaknesses as a plan centerpiece, disabling the boot pair and SYNC row is a settings-only action that closes the boot race *today* — whereas claude's plan leaves that race live through the capture and shadow phases. The synthesis should adopt an explicit interim precaution (even just "maintainer disables SYNC SAVES DURING STARTUP until #22 lands").
10. **The concession ledger as a required artifact** — documenting which Step-1 claims were refuted and why. Claude has its own ledger, so this is partially absorbed, but gemini's is the model of the form.

**From `mistral-revised_plan.md`:**

11. **The explicit allowlist rule for any retention store under `savestates/`** (`- /savestates/.discards/**` ahead of `+ /savestates/**`, first-match-wins). Claude places its discard store outside the sync tree (better), but if any retention or snapshot store ever lands under `savestates/`, mistral's placement rule is the guard; claude covers `.snapshots` via `--exclude` flags but never states the discard-store-in-savestates case.
12. **`container` as a first-class schema `kind`** — a schema-shape alternative to claude's `unit` field, worth preserving for the D-CLOUD-031 refinement discussion.
13. **Kid/kiosk wizard reachability as a hardware proof item.** Claude decides kid-mode behavior (§3.5.6) but its §7 hardware steps never test it; mistral's proof list does.
14. **"Hook the move operations instead of building occurrence identifiers"** — a scoping discipline worth stating explicitly as the reason a second identity system is out.

## Remaining defects in the winner to fix before building

1. **The residual lost-update window (§3.7.4).** Row 4 can still silently download a racing winner over the loser's newer local progress; recovery is manual, and the reconcile's *download* path is not clearly given the same `--backup-dir` belt the upload path gets (§3.7.3 says "every cloud overwrite"). Fix: retain replaced local files on download too, specify the anomaly report's exact wording and timing, and either adopt a lighter form of gpt's protected publications or explicitly record the window as accepted.
2. **Ambiguous lock semantics across the walkthrough.** "One lock held across plan and apply" (§3.3.2) versus the apply mode "takes it again" — resolve to release-during-walkthrough with the COMPLETE-time revalidation the plan already has.
3. **Interim exposure.** The capture and shadow phases leave the boot race live for weeks; prescribe the interim operating precaution (startup toggle off) or pull the boot-race fix ahead of shadow mode.
4. **V1 slicing.** The F/R table helps, but the build order *within* the F items (which conflict-table rows ship first, whether shadow mode precedes the gated exit push) needs one more pass to keep the milestone deliverable by a small team.
5. **Deletion verification on hashless backends.** Row 9's archive-then-delete must download-and-hash before deleting where no native hash exists; the plan's provisional rules cover classification but the deletion path's cost and test on the QA WebDAV should be made explicit.
6. **The `--files-from` listing mechanism** for the gated exit push is asserted across backends; promote it to a spike contract item with a named fallback, and note the keep-discarded default-ON proposal contradicts IA rev 4's settled section (it is properly flagged as a new register row, but the maintainer should see the collision stated).
