**Winner: `gpt-revised_plan-r3.md`.**

## Reasoning

Two of the four plans are standalone foundations a builder could work from; two are not.

**`mistral-revised_plan-r3.md`** opens by declaring itself an edit of `claude-revised_plan-r2.md`, which is not in the corpus, and its §12 costs are stated against r2 plans the maintainer cannot read. Beyond that, it carries a sentence that reads as a silent-overwrite path: §4.3 and its proposed D-CLOUD-029-A say that if hashless verification exceeds the budget, "defer verification to the boot/menu full pass, leaving the exit push as a fast-path upload only." Every other plan says the *upload* is deferred; mistral's wording permits pushing over an unread head and calling it deferred verification. It also proposes "Spawn A" (a local backend-hash computation, `busybox md5sum` on md5 backends) on the watched exit path while budgeting 3–4 spawns there — against D-CLOUD-028's one-spawn/~1 s measurements in `_sources/repo/.claude/rules/rclone-cloud-sync.md`; Dropbox's own hash type has no busybox equivalent anyway. §7.1's "a dated directory under the sync root, never inside it" is self-contradictory. Its schema section is largely `_sources/repo/docs/save-manifest-schema.md` restated. Fourth.

**`gemini-revised_plan-r3.md`** is substantively sound on the hard rules — member-map classification, strict hashless verification, read-before-upload, `--files-from`, tombstones for explicit deletes, per-unit retention with exemptions — but it is an adoption list, not a foundation. Nearly every mechanism is specified by pointer ("Adopted: `gpt-revised_plan-r2.md`'s requirement…", "the `gemini` fallback") to documents the builder does not have. Where it does specify, it is thin: §3.5 names `getNextFreeSlot()` as "correctly" returning −99 for auto-only repositories (it is a defect the adapter must absorb, per `_sources/es/SaveStateRepository.cpp`) and never mentions that `copyToSlot()` discards the copy/rename results (`_sources/es/SaveState.cpp`); its store is keyed by `<path>/`, which renumbering moves under the player's feet; its lifecycle-lock sketch covers snapshot reads but not installs; it surfaces no gaps to the orchestrator. Third.

**`kimi-revised_plan-r3.md` versus `gpt-revised_plan-r3.md`** is close, and kimi is the more concrete and compact document — a builder would find kimi easier to start from. But the brief orders progress protection first, grounding second, buildability third, and gpt is stronger on the first two:

1. **Hashless backends.** gpt §6.1 refuses manifest claims, size and mtime as overwrite authority outright: they direct the investigation, they never certify the cloud's current bytes. kimi §3.1 seeds agreement on a hashless backend from "a matching foreign-manifest claim with matching size+mtime," and its exit pass (§3.3 step 3) confirms C = A from listing plus claim before uploading. That is rclone's own default comparison level, which is exactly what produced #53 (`_sources/repo/projects/ROCKNIX/packages/network/rclone/sources/cloud_backup`, the `backup_system_files` comment). gpt's rule costs a scoped download of tens of KB; kimi's saves it at the price of a heuristic on the one class of backend the QA suite exists to exercise (`_sources/repo/docs/save-manifest-alignment-review.md` §1, D-QA-001/002 row). Gemini adopted gpt's rule; kimi did not.
2. **Capture is independent of the sync setting.** gpt §3.3 requires capture when exit sync is disabled, offline, lock held, or the emulator exited non-zero. kimi §3.2 hooks capture "at the existing exit point [S41]" — but that call is inside `if (cloudsaves.gameexit == "1" && … && !ThreadedCloudSync::isRunning())` in `_sources/es/FileData.cpp.launchGame-excerpt-l740-850.cpp`. Adopted as written, kimi loses provenance for every save written while the toggle is off or a sync is running, and those files arrive at the wizard as `unknown`.
3. **Gate ownership across ES death.** gpt §3.2 names the hazard — ES dies, the emulator lives, an ES-owned marker vanishes — and requires the launch supervisor or emulator process lifetime to own the lifecycle gate. kimi §1 item 9 specifies acquisition order well but not who holds the gate if ES is not there to hold it.
4. **Retention that survives renumbering and wrong clocks.** gpt §8.4 buckets renumberable numbered states by game/core rather than by slot path, prunes by local commit order, and keeps routine one-way preimages out of the conflict-discard allowance. kimi's store is per operation-id with per-unit counts and a "no wall-clock uniqueness" note, but its prune order is unstated, and "per save unit" for a numbered state is per path — the same instability D-CLOUD-030 exists to escape. gemini's store is path-keyed outright.
5. **Retirement intent outlives the discard count.** gpt §5.2: notices are current intent, not pruned by the discard history; hitting a limit stops further automatic retirement. kimi §3.7 prunes tombstones with the retention window and accepts that a returning device resurrects the file — honestly stated, but a convergence hole gpt closes.
6. **Reader semantics.** gpt §4.2 makes the union of manifests a set of claims rather than last-map-wins for a path, separates manifest owner from byte producer, and quarantines foreign manifests in an out-of-tree observation cache so they are never republished. kimi §3.1 has the coherent-member-map rule but not the owner/producer distinction.

On grounding I checked every source-visible claim gpt makes against the embedded files and found them correct: the racommands/autosave/incremental flip when any `es_savestates.cfg` is parsed (`_sources/es/SaveStateConfigFile.cpp`, `emul->racommands = false`); `setupSaveState`'s `.state.auto` → `.bak` dance and its restore in `onGameEnded` (so "auto diverges every session" is not established for numbered-slot launches — `_sources/es/SaveState.cpp`); the −99 allocator result and `copyToSlot`'s unconditional `true`; raw rclone exit codes 3/4 colliding with the UI's SKIPPED meanings (`cloud_backup` `clean_exit`, `_sources/es/ThreadedCloudSync.cpp`); the last-phase exit masking a system-phase failure; every harness defect in §10.3 (`_sources/repo/tools/cloud-round-trip` writes `rclone.conf` before asserting the remote and never restores it, expects `{device_id}/{ARCHIVE_NAME}` where the uploader stamps `${stamp}-${base}`, reads the archive back at the undated path where `cloud_restore` keeps the dated name, and looks for a `*_BACKUP.tar.gz` its fixture never writes). kimi is nearly as well grounded but has one slip: §3.7 justifies not hooking `SaveState::remove()` because "`onGameEnded` also calls [it] for its own cleanup [S38]" — `onGameEnded` calls `Utils::FileSystem::removeFile` directly, not `remove()`. The conclusion (hook the `GuiSaveState` delete action) is still right; the argument is not.

On buildability kimi wins: twelve sentences, a literal rclone command, a literal store layout. gpt is three times longer and more abstract in places. But gpt bounds its persistent machinery explicitly (§11 "Explicitly not V1": no DAG, no SQLite, no daemon, no receipts, no move hooks) and its extra components — sync-context binding, the observation cache, run-bound result records — are each a few fields or one directory, not subsystems. On sequencing both have ordered gates; gpt's §10.1 table names owner, blocking step and pass condition per gate, and its §10.4 table of cheapest experiments (same basename in two ROM directories mapping to one state path; case folding; stale thumbnail with changed state; `.bak` leakage during a session; cloned card; staging on a different filesystem; wrong clock pruning the newest discard) has no equivalent elsewhere. On honesty gpt's §9.4 register table names which rows it asks to refine and lists those it does not reopen, and labels its proposed rows as labels rather than IDs.

## What the winner needs to satisfy the amendments

It already conforms: V1 recovery route withdrawn (§1), retention on by default and count-bounded (§8.1), no undo control in the resolution flow, done page names discards and says they are kept on this device, the store record is self-contained and independent of the manifest, the audit log and the apply plan (§8.3), and the history-restore tool is a separate issue (§8.5). Nothing more must be given up on undo.

Three things still need settling against the amendments:

- **Store location must be decided with the shape.** gpt proposes `/storage/.local/share/rocknix/cloud-saves/`, a path convention that appears nowhere in the corpus; whether `backuptool` archives it and whether any cleanup touches it are unverified (gpt says so). The corpus precedent is that `/storage/.cache/log/` already holds the non-regenerable audit log (D-CLOUD-027, `_sources/repo/docs/decision-register.md`). Either confirm the new path or take kimi's `.cache/cloud_sync/discarded/` with the explicit "this subtree is not a cache" rule — a maintainer call, recorded in the same register row as the store shape.
- **Trim the store to the primary case.** gpt's retention reasons include "redundant occurrence retirement" (D-CLOUD-030 compaction). Compaction is lossless by construction — the identical bytes survive at the lower slot — so those copies should be audit-only (kimi §3.6), not entries that can crowd a real loser out of a bucket. Keeping one preimage for a routine one-way replacement is within one step back and should stay, bounded to one, as gpt already separates it.
- **The reopening of D-CLOUD-014/015 needs a plainer statement.** "Default upgraded configurations continue on guarded sync" is ambiguous. The needed sentence is: once the engine owns the saves tier, `BACKUPMETHOD` no longer governs it; a legacy `sync` value authorizes nothing the classifier did not approve. That is a consequence of the engine, not a reversal of the rows.

## Remaining defects in the winner, to fix before it is built

1. **Standalone-emulator capture selection is under-specified.** §3.3 says "hash supported session outputs, identify newly encountered files" and forbids a ROM-basename filter (right, per D-CLOUD-028) and mtime as authority (right). It does not say how the changed set is bounded on a large save tree without hashing everything on every exit. mtime as a *candidate hint* with hash as the decision is the obvious answer; say it, and let gate 5 price it.
2. **Retirement-notice transport is implied, not stated.** §4.1, §4.5 and §5.2 together imply notices live in the owned manifest; one sentence must say so, with the bound and the stop-rule when the bound is reached.
3. **Gate ownership when the launch plumbing is not yet inspected.** §3.2 correctly requires the emulator lifetime to own the gate and defers the mechanism to inspection. Until then, the worker must treat an indeterminate gate as *held* — fail closed. The plan should state that default.
4. **The store path convention** (above) — an unverified new directory under `/storage` cannot be the resting place of the only recoverable copy until `backuptool`'s archive set and any cleanup routines have been read.
5. **Compaction copies in the store** (above) — remove "redundant occurrence retirement" from the retained-copy reasons or exclude it from the count.
6. **D-CLOUD-014/015 wording** (above).
7. **Length.** As a foundation it is complete; as a build brief it needs kimi's twelve-sentence front matter and gemini's section-7 "what should not be built" concision, so the load-bearing rules are not buried under the proof program.
