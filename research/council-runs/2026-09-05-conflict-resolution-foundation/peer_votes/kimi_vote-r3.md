**Winner: `gpt-revised_plan-r3.md`.**

## Reasoning

All four plans converge on the same safe skeleton — content-hash identity, a three-way classifier owning every write path, fresh cloud evidence before any overwrite, retention on by default with no V1 undo control, a checked adapter over ES's defective slot primitives, and cheap gates before expensive building. The vote therefore turns on which plan is safest and most buildable *as written*, and on the quality of each plan's grounding against the corpus.

**Against `mistral-revised_plan-r3.md`:** this is an editing proposal for someone else's plan, not a foundation. Its §3 restates the signed schema wholesale (padding that adds risk, not value), and its one original schema contribution is malformed: a `kind: "container"` entry keyed on `dc/shared/savefiles/` — a directory path carrying a `sha256` of nothing definable. Its decision-register amendments are mislabeled: "D-CLOUD-029-A" contains exit-path evidence content that belongs to D-CLOUD-028 (D-CLOUD-029 is the no-`--update`-stopgap row), and "D-CLOUD-027-A" attaches the discard store to the audit-log row. It references "Gate 6" without defining any gate program of its own, and its deletion section says retirement siblings are "a dated directory under the sync root, never inside it" — self-contradictory. Adopted as written, it would need another plan to fill in before anyone could build from it.

**Against `gemini-revised_plan-r3.md`:** a sound, honest plan, but thinner where the corpus demands precision. Its merge story adopts `getNextFreeSlot()` and `copyToSlot(move=false)` while addressing only the −99 auto-only defect — it never handles `copyToSlot()`'s unconditional `true` return or `makeStateFilename(fullPath)` deriving the destination from the *source's* parent directory, both source-visible in `es/SaveState.cpp`. A KEEP BOTH built on §3.5 as written could report success while writing into the staging directory. Its lifecycle gate section claims to address ES-death survival but describes only a snapshot-lock ordering that does not. Its discard store is keyed by `<path>`, which scatters retained copies across renumbering — the exact instability D-CLOUD-030 exists to abstract over. Its register work stops at three rows and it has no harness-repair analysis.

**Against `claude-revised_plan-r3.md`:** the strongest runner-up, and in several areas richer than the winner — a concrete lifecycle mechanism (flock with the fd inherited by the emulator child, flagged `[K]` with a named fallback), a four-phase pending-apply record, the best amendment-compliance test in the field (Gate 8's test-only reader after manifest overwrite, renumber, log rotation and plan removal), a named churn-trace convergence gate, and precise spawn accounting (0 idle / 3–4 changed) with a pre-registered degrade order. It loses on three points. First, **budget risk on the watched path**: four rclone spawns per changed exit, one of them buying a manifest-last "commit point" that `gpt-revised_plan-r3.md` correctly argues is not load-bearing — a manifest is an observation, and readers must verify described bytes and treat mismatches as pending regardless, so the fourth spawn purchases ceremony. Second, **buildability**: more machinery (resolves receipts, retired ring, `writer_instance`, O1's `--order-by` dependency) means more that can drift, even with optional items marked optional. Third, **instrument repair**: every plan gates everything on `tools/cloud-round-trip`, but only `gpt-revised_plan-r3.md` demonstrates the harness is currently broken in specific, source-verifiable ways.

That last point deserves emphasis because it is the grounding differentiator. I checked each of GPT's harness claims against `repo/tools/cloud-round-trip` and the shipped scripts, and all hold: the harness overwrites `rclone.conf` and never restores it in cleanup; its namespacing assertion `f"{device_id}/{ARCHIVE_NAME}" in listing` cannot match the dated upload name `cloud_backup` actually writes (`${stamp}-${base}` for undated archives, per `cloud_backup`); it then checks the restored archive at the *undated* path while `cloud_restore` deliberately keeps the remote's dated name (`local_name="${newest}"`), guaranteeing a spurious FAIL; its tar.gz step lists `/storage/roms/backup/` for an archive its own zip fixture never creates; and most invocations omit `--yes` with no subprocess timeout, so a `controller_confirm` prompt would hang forever. A plan that begins by repairing the instrument everyone else assumes works is the plan whose subsequent gate results can be trusted. That is *verify the artifact, not the report* applied to the test suite itself, and it is exactly the discipline `repo/docs/blindspot-register.md` entries 13 and 22 demand.

GPT also wins two design points outright. Its **retention store** is the best answer to the maintainer's future-reader requirement: per-bucket counts (in-game unit, auto resume point, and a game/core collection bucket for numbered states so renumbering cannot scatter or strand a game's recovery copies), pruning by local commit order rather than wall clock (safe against a wrong RTC), transactional preimages held separately from conflict discards, an explicit field-by-field record contract, and an explicit affirmative analysis that a later picker can be driven from the store alone. Its **register honesty** is the most precise: it is the only plan that names the signed schema's actual gaps — transfer-only agreement (so a device upgraded with L = C everywhere gets a false "never agreed → ask" conflict on its first edit, violating `upgrade-and-install.md`'s invisibility rule) and last-map-wins union semantics — and requests a D-CLOUD-031 refinement with justification, rather than only appending additive rows.

## What the winner needs to satisfy the amendments

Very little — it is already compliant: retention on by default, count-bounded, no undo control in the resolution flow, the done page naming discarded copies and stating they are kept, the store designed for a reader that does not exist, and the history-restore tool re-homed to a separate issue shaped as the compare-and-choose surface pointed at retained versions. Two confirmations only: (a) the IA document itself must receive the rev-5 amendments it lists (queued unattended conflicts, cancellation wording, done-page sentence), not just the plan's register table; (b) its proposed `/storage/.local/share/rocknix/cloud-saves/` home must pass its own named acceptance tests — upgrade survival and exclusion from backup and cleanup tooling — or fall back to the exempted `.cache` placement it explicitly keeps open. Nothing needs to be given up; it already withdrew its V1 native-recovery surface.

## Dissent — primitives from losing plans the winner does not fully absorb

From **`claude-revised_plan-r3.md`**:
- The **concrete lifecycle mechanism**: `flock` on a lifecycle gate with the fd inherited by the emulator child so ES's death (a documented SIGABRT-restart pattern in `engineering-practices.md`) does not release it mid-session, with a wrapper fallback if `ProcessStartInfo` closes inherited fds. GPT names the requirement but defers the mechanism.
- The **four-phase pending-apply record** (`prepared`/`installed`/`published`/`committed`) with per-phase recovery semantics — sharper than GPT's frozen-plan journal.
- **Gate 8's retained-store readability proof**: a test-only reader driven after the producer's manifest entry is overwritten, slots renumbered, the audit log rotated and the plan file removed. GPT's Gate 0 fixture is cheaper but weaker; the synthesis should carry both.
- The **churn-trace convergence gate** (cloud-only `state5` onto `{0,1,2}`, download, launch/exit, sync twice → one copy per side) — the only named experiment that proves compaction actually terminates.
- The **0-idle / ≤4-changed spawn accounting with a pre-registered degrade order** (drop the verify listing, then O1; the preflight is never dropped) — GPT's exit-path accounting is looser and should adopt this so its Gate 5 measurement has a budget to measure against.
- The **`resolves` one-step receipt** (O6, optional): without it, a reflashed or fresh device re-asks about conflicts the player already decided. GPT's conservative-ask outcome is safe but more prompting; this deserves an explicit maintainer call rather than silent omission.
- The **restore-tool shape detail**: the copy a restore replaces becomes a *new* retained record, making a wrong restore itself one-step reversible — a sharper guarantee than GPT's "restore as a new publication."
- The **mass-absence refusal threshold** (more than N previously agreed units absent at once refuses the whole pass) — more explicit than GPT's "hold mutation."
- **Option hygiene specifics**: compose the reconciler's own options, never pass `RCLONEOPTS` raw, and `--exclude '*.bak'` on every transfer (`.state.auto.bak` passes `+ /**/*.state*` while matching neither ES regex — source-visible across `cloud_sync-rules.txt`, `es/SaveState.cpp`, `es/SaveStateConfigFile.cpp`).

From **`gemini-revised_plan-r3.md`**: little unique survives — its equality-establishes-agreement rule, LAN-route reachability, hashless-deferral fallback and `--files-from` transport are all absorbed by the winner. Its simple done-page wording ("Discarded copies are kept and can be recovered later") is worth lifting verbatim.

From **`mistral-revised_plan-r3.md`**: the per-plan "what the amendments cost" accounting (§12) is a useful synthesis artifact for the orchestrator. Its `kind: "container"` should **not** be absorbed — GPT's unit-level container distinction is strictly better, and mistral's directory-keyed example is malformed. Its one sharp warning — a retention cap must stop mutation rather than silently drop records — is already present in the winner.

## Remaining defects in the winner to fix before building

1. **The lifecycle gate is unspecified at the mechanism level.** Adopt claude's flock-plus-fd-inheritance design (both plans name the same `ProcessStartInfo` inspection to verify it) before the write-path cutover; without it, the boot pass can still mutate a tree a running emulator is writing.
2. **Torn-publication handling rests on reader-side "manifest/payload mismatch → pending."** Its own 10.4 reorder/interruption experiment must be promoted from table row to release gate; if the experiment shows ambiguity, claude's manifest-last ordering is the fallback worth its one spawn.
3. **The retained-store home decision must be made before #22, not during it**, with the cleanup-tooling exemption written and proven either way — a store the OS can legitimately delete is the one failure the maintainer's amendment explicitly forecloses.
4. **The fresh-device re-ask trade-off** (no `resolves` receipts) should be recorded as a conscious decision, not an accident of omission.
5. **The harness repair it correctly specifies must actually happen first** — including restoring the device's `rclone.conf` — and not be deferred once the suite starts mostly passing (blindspot 13: a repaired instrument is only evidence once its failures have been observed to fire).
