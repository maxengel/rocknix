# Vote: **claude**

## Why claude's revised plan is strongest

**1. It resolves the device-gate controversy with a mechanism, not a deferral.** The hardest open question across all four plans is what 0.0.1 actually ships. Gemini mandates flashing every physical target (maximum owner yeses on the critical path). Muse keeps an x64-only fallback that ships nothing a handheld owner can use. GPT recommends a scope but leaves the mechanics to the owner. Claude is the only plan that converts the dispute into an executable compromise: x64-first *qualification*, one canonical tag, **one** mandatory migration rehearsal on a real RC2 handheld (the test that actually matters — an upgrade with live saves, not a VM), and three owner-scheduled flashes with explicit "built from the same manifest, not yet device-verified" disclosure and a stated 0.0.2 verification date. That preserves the migration guarantee while cutting the critical-path yes budget, and it honestly prices the residual risk instead of hiding it.

**2. It is the only plan that prices the frame everyone else optimizes inside.** Every other plan assumes upstream tracking and argues about merge-cost management within it. Claude's §2.4 puts the pin-vs-track decision itself on the books: track by default, with divergence and patch-refresh metrics reviewed at cadence, a pre-declared budget, and a trigger that re-opens the pin decision — and, crucially, couples the code-level rename reconsideration to the same trigger. That turns two separate "defer forever vs. decide now" arguments into one decision with an exit. This is the kind of structural move that survives contact with reality, because it defines in advance what evidence would change the plan's mind.

**3. Estimative realism.** The corpus's own cold-build sample (~24h per root) means four serialized targets consume roughly four days of wall time before any rehearsal, QA, or owner latency. Claude's 12–18 elapsed days is the only estimate that is arithmetically consistent with that sample. GPT's 4–7 day window assumes "one to two days of aggregate serial build time," which contradicts the sample it elsewhere treats as load-bearing; muse's 3–5 days is further off still. Claude also carries the labour/elapsed/yeses triple through every phase rather than asserting it once — the exact discipline the reviews demanded.

**4. Exit criteria are runnable and falsifiable.** Non-printing isolation assertions (`test -r` returning 1, not `cat`), a canary with an alert, a negative-control frame that *must fail* template matching, truncated-download and mid-install-interrupt recovery cases, every check bound to a manifest digest. These are tests a reader can execute and a result that can fail — not checklist aspirations.

**5. Completeness without empire-building.** It absorbs the long tail (redirect sunset criterion, 2FA custody correlation, prior-identity debris, ES localization orphaning, release-channel discipline, trust-assumption statement for the unsigned channel) while explicitly rejecting the heavy machinery (five-role separation, formal threat model, manifest service, signing implementation — slot designed only). The owner questions are reduced to five that have no technical answer.

## Dissent notes — primitives the winner does not fully absorb

- **GPT's updater timebox.** Claude's Branch A/B decision rule bounds the updater work implicitly, but GPT's explicit budget — one session to understand the mechanism, one to attempt the minimal correction, then manual adoption — is a sharper guard against silent scope expansion on the single highest-uncertainty work item. Claude should adopt it verbatim.
- **GPT's estimate invalidation conditions.** Claude's estimates are conditional but don't state the predicates that void them. GPT's "unresolved source obligations, repository access, or a substantial updater rewrite explicitly invalidate the window" makes the estimate falsifiable rather than merely hedged.
- **GPT's requalification rule.** If an ARM fix changes frozen shared inputs, the x64 candidate must be requalified — "one head" must not conceal different dependency revisions. Claude's manifest binding gets halfway there but never states the requalification consequence.
- **GPT's owner-unavailable stop rule and owner-hours budget.** Claude counts yeses but not hours, and lacks the explicit "stop at the immutable candidate; do not expose an incomplete release" halting condition.
- **Muse's P0 read list** is marginally the most comprehensive (asset-size headroom and BUILD_ID derivation as named reads), though claude absorbs both elsewhere.
- **Gemini stands as a caution**: its revised plan dropped backup/restore entirely despite having championed disaster recovery in review — a reminder that correction lists and plan bodies drift apart, and claude's plan should be checked for the same drift before execution.

**Ranking:** claude > gpt > muse > gemini. GPT is a close second on proportionality and epistemic discipline; it loses on estimative consistency and on leaving the publish gate as a recommendation rather than a mechanism.
